VWO: 17% earnings growth and 7% returns

Why VWO can show a gap between underlying earnings growth and past returns.

Why VWO can show a gap between underlying earnings growth and past returns.

What VWO owns

VWO gives an investor a broad emerging-markets wrapper, with major positions including TSMC, Tencent, Alibaba and Reliance Industries. The video notes a low expense ratio of about 0.08%, but argues that a low fee alone does not answer whether the fund is attractive.

Valuation and growth can diverge

The comparison in the episode contrasts an S&P 500 valuation around 30 times earnings with VWO around 15 times, while also noting strong earnings growth for the fund’s holdings over the preceding five years. Lower valuation and faster earnings do not guarantee higher returns, especially when currency, country risk and sector composition differ.

Use the ETF as an allocation decision

Review the holdings, regional exposures, fund structure and role in a wider portfolio. The right question is whether emerging-market exposure improves the investor’s overall diversification and expected return, not whether one headline multiple is cheaper.
