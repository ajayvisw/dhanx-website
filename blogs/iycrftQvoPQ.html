A concentrated portfolio, explained

The video uses the Nevada Investment Fund as a case study in concentrated ownership. Between 2001 and 2014, its holdings in Costco, Amazon and Berkshire Hathaway reportedly produced annualised returns of about 20.8%, compared with roughly 6.5% for the benchmark cited in the video.

What the example does and does not prove

The lesson is not that three stocks are always safer or that past concentration should be copied. The case illustrates why an investor may focus on businesses with durable advantages, capable management and a long runway for compounding. Concentration magnifies both insight and error, so the burden of research rises with position size.
