# Five fault lines, and why I am holding more cash

For the first time in roughly fifteen years of investing, I am deliberately holding
a significantly higher cash position.

That is not a crash call. I do not know that one is coming. It is an observation
that several fragile things are developing at once, and that they could reinforce
one another over a six-month to three-year horizon. In that situation, dry powder
is worth holding.

## AI capital expenditure and private credit

Enormous sums are flowing into AI infrastructure, data centres and neoclouds, much
of it raised from private equity rather than public markets.

CoreWeave carries roughly **$35 billion of debt**. Nebius and others are in similar
territory. If the economics of AI shift, the return profile deteriorates — and the
effect does not stop at the companies.

Investors in the private funds behind them face redemption limits. That pattern has
already appeared elsewhere: the Blackstone REIT episode, where investors could not
withdraw freely and the fund repriced. The illiquidity is a feature of the
structure, and it becomes visible precisely when people want out.

## Token economics

Inference costs keep falling. OpenAI cut pricing on an older model by roughly
**80%**.

Excellent for customers. Harder for the companies funding frontier development. Even
if volume rises, a lower average selling price compresses margin, and the return on
the capital deployed weakens. That matters particularly for companies considering
public listings in the next year or so.

## Interest rates

Treasury yields near **5%** and mortgage rates around **7%** change behaviour.

They pressure government borrowing costs, they slow home buying, and they weigh on
asset valuations generally. Small home builders look like the most exposed point in
US real estate over the next six months to two years.

## Hyperscaler capital expenditure

Amazon, Meta and the other large technology companies are funding this from free
cash flow rather than debt, which makes them far more robust than the neoclouds.

They remain excellent businesses. But the return on the investment still has to
materialise, and the present rate of spending is almost certainly an overshoot.
Write-downs and bad debt should be expected rather than treated as a surprise.

## Oil and geopolitics

Sustained oil above **$100 a barrel** feeds inflation, complicates the path for
interest rates, and constrains the global economy.

## Why cash, rather than a prediction

None of these is individually a crisis, and each has a benign resolution. The
concern is that they are correlated: falling AI returns pressure private credit;
higher rates make refinancing harder; an oil shock removes the option of cutting
rates to compensate.

Holding cash is not a forecast that assets must fall. It is a decision to keep
flexibility when valuations, leverage and investment intensity all look demanding at
the same time — and to have something available if the opportunity arrives.

*Personal views, educational only, and not financial advice.*
