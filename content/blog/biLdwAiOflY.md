# TSMC: the company every AI winner depends on

There are only a handful of trillion-dollar companies outside the US west coast.
TSMC is one of them, and it sits underneath nearly every outcome in AI.

It makes chips for Apple, Nvidia, AMD and Qualcomm. Your iPhone runs on TSMC
silicon, and so do the models you query.

## The market position

- Approximately **60%** of the global foundry market.
- Roughly **90%** of advanced manufacturing at the **5nm and 3nm** nodes.
- Major footprint in Hsinchu, Taiwan, expanding into Arizona, Germany and Japan.

The AI debate is usually about which company wins — Nvidia, Amazon, Google. All of
them depend on TSMC. That makes it the hedge on a question nobody can answer: you
do not need to pick the winner if you own the supplier to all of them.

## The financials

Revenue grew from **$20 billion to $88 billion** over ten years. Return on equity
around **27%**. Margins near **40%**. Dividend yield about **1.1%**.

Valuation is not cheap. Price to earnings around **30**, price to free cash flow
about **47** — the latter inflated by enormous capital expenditure during the AI
build-out.

Fairly priced, in my view. Cheap, certainly not.

## The moat

TSMC has effectively no competition at the leading edge, and the reasons compound.

Engineering capability at the bleeding edge is genuinely hard to replicate. Yields
at scale are better than anyone else's. And critically, **TSMC does not compete
with its customers** — a foundry that also designs chips asks its customers to hand
designs to a rival. That trust is a structural advantage Samsung cannot simply buy.

## The risk, stated honestly

Geopolitics. Taiwan is the obvious vulnerability and it should not be dismissed.

The counter-argument is that TSMC is a golden goose. The global semiconductor and
electronics supply chain runs through Taiwan, which gives it a **silicon shield** —
the asset is only valuable intact, and annexation would destroy what made it worth
taking. That assumes rational actors on all sides, which is an assumption rather
than a guarantee.

## A deliberately conservative valuation

The high-performance computing segment is growing at roughly **60% annually** and
contributes about **53%** of revenue.

Now sandbag it. Assume HPC grows at only **30%** going forward, and assume **the
entire rest of the company is flat at 0%**.

The weighted average over five years is still about **21%** — meaning revenue
roughly doubles in under three and a half years, from around $90 billion to about
**$212 billion**.

At a **35% net margin**, that is approximately **$75 billion of earnings**. Apply a
**25× multiple** — well below the current one — and the company is worth around
**$1.8 trillion** against a present valuation near **$1.5 trillion**.

## Why sandbag at every step

Because the AI spending boom will end. Capital expenditure of $100 billion a year
and rising is not sustainable indefinitely, and earnings have to catch up with it
within three to five years.

When gravity applies, TSMC is affected. That is precisely why the model should not
assume 45% or 55% growth in high-performance computing. If the conservative case
still clears the current price, the investment does not depend on the boom
continuing.

**If AI is the new electricity, TSMC is the power grid.**
