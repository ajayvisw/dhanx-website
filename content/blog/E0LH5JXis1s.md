# NSE IPO: five things to understand before applying

NSE is coming to market at roughly **₹4.4 lakh crore** — about **$46 billion** — at
approximately **43× FY26 earnings**. Five things worth knowing.

## 1. It is genuinely dominant

NSE processes a very large share of Indian equity trading. Whatever broking app sits
in front of a retail investor, the trade is executed on NSE or BSE.

## 2. Revenue is heavily concentrated in options

**77% of revenue comes from the options market** — close to ₹10,000 crore of roughly
₹13,000 crore total in FY26.

One product, one regulator, three-quarters of the business.

## 3. That product has a problem

SEBI's analysis found approximately **93% of derivatives traders lost money**, with
aggregate losses around **₹1.8 lakh crore** over three years.

A complete ban is unlikely — it is too lucrative for exchanges and governments
alike. But continued tightening of entry barriers and product gating is close to
certain, and each intervention lands on the revenue line that matters most.

## 4. BSE is taking share where it counts

Daily options turnover: NSE around **₹57,000 crore**, BSE about **₹19,000 crore**.

BSE holds roughly **25%** and is growing fast. Moving to 30–35% would be material
for BSE — and it comes out of NSE's largest pool.

## 5. The valuation requires the growth to arrive

43× is not unreasonable for a business compounding 15–20%, and it is not cheap.

The quality is not in doubt. NSE will exist as long as Indian equity markets do, and
no new entrant will replicate its liquidity depth. The risk is not the business
failing — it is paying a full price and then waiting. Microsoft returned roughly
nothing between 2001 and 2013 while remaining an excellent company.

## The question

Great business, certainly. Expensive stock depends entirely on how much future
growth is already in the price.
