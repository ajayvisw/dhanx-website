# The Costco story, in brief

A company Charlie Munger admired enough to hold around $150 million of. I have never
owned a share, which I regret every time I walk into one.

## Price Club, 1976

Sol Price, a lawyer turned entrepreneur, opened Price Club in San Diego — a no-frills
warehouse where small businesses bought in bulk at near-wholesale prices.

The formula: **low prices, high volume, absolute integrity.**

## Costco, 1983

Jim Sinegal, an early Price Club employee who had learned the wholesale trade from
Sol Price, opened the first Costco with Jeff Brotman in SoDo, south of downtown
Seattle.

Same ethos: unbeatable value, fair treatment of employees, customers treated as
family.

## The merger, 1993

Both chains were thriving when they combined in a **$2 billion merger** to form
Price Costco. Within a year the Price family had sold its entire stake, and the
company became Costco Wholesale.

## Four facts that explain the model

**The $1.50 hot dog** has held that price since 1983. It is a statement of intent
rather than a product decision.

**No product is marked up more than 15%.** Buy something at Costco and you know it
was procured for at least 85% of what you paid. That cap is a promise the company
has chosen not to break.

**Around 3,700 SKUs** per warehouse, against roughly **100,000** at a comparable
Walmart. Fewer choices means more volume per item, which means better buying power,
which means lower prices. The restriction is the strategy.

**Kirkland Signature generates about $85 billion** annually — roughly **33% of
Costco's $250 billion** revenue.

## Why it is hard to copy

Every element works against conventional retail instinct: fewer products, capped
margins, higher wages. Each one individually looks like money left on the table.

Together they produce something a competitor cannot replicate by matching one of
them — a customer who believes the price is fair without checking. That trust is
the asset, and it was built by declining to exploit it for forty years.

Growth achieved without greed, at low prices, with high ethics and loyal employees.
Rare enough to be worth studying.
