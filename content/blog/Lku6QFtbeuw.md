# VWO: 17% earnings growth, 7% returns

Vanguard's FTSE Emerging Markets ETF has lagged the S&P 500 over one, three and
five years. Its underlying earnings have not.

## What you own

TSMC, Tencent, Alibaba, Reliance, ICICI, Infosys, HDFC, Foxconn — the significant
businesses outside the United States.

Over **$100 billion in assets** at an expense ratio of about **0.08%**. Efficient
and large.

## The disconnect

**Trailing P/E**: roughly **15×** for VWO against approximately **30×** for the S&P
500.

**Earnings growth**: about **17.5% compounded** over five years.

**ETF return**: a little over **7.5%**.

Earnings grew at more than double the rate the price did. The multiple absorbed the
difference — investors paid progressively less for each unit of earnings, which is
the definition of a widening gap between price and value.

## Why it is overlooked

Delisting fears around Chinese holdings account for much of the discount, and they
look overdone relative to the breadth of what the fund holds — this is not a China
fund.

More simply: emerging markets have been uninteresting while US technology has been
compelling. Attention follows recent returns, and valuations follow attention.

## The case

You do not make money chasing hype. You make it buying value — and a basket of
real businesses compounding earnings at 17.5% while priced at half the US multiple
is where value tends to be found.

Whether the gap closes, and when, is unknown. But a coiled spring is a reasonable
description.
