# Worked in the US and moved back to India? Claim your Social Security

If you worked in the United States for more than ten years and accumulated **40
Social Security credits**, you are eligible for monthly payments for life —
regardless of where you now live.

Many people who have returned to India do not realise this, and the sums involved
are not small.

## The eligibility rule

**40 credits, earned over roughly ten years** of work with Social Security
contributions.

Moving home does not forfeit them. The entitlement follows the contributions, not
your current address. A short list of countries — Cuba and North Korea among them —
are excluded; for most of the world, including India, payment abroad is permitted.
The SSA publishes
[the rules for receiving payments outside the US](https://www.ssa.gov/international/payments.html).

## The first step

Go to **ssa.gov**, create a my Social Security account, and download your
statement.

It shows what you would receive at **62**, at **full retirement age**, and at
**70**. These are your actual numbers rather than estimates — worth looking at
before making any assumptions.

## The claiming decision

Benefits rise the longer you wait. Illustratively: about **$2,600 a month at 62**,
rising to roughly **$3,900 at 67** and **$4,900 at 70**.

The conventional advice is to wait for the larger cheque. I think that is often
wrong, for a reason the conventional framing ignores.

**Claiming at 62 and investing the payments** changes the comparison. At a
hypothetical 10% return, the money drawn from 62 compounds for eight years before
the person who waited receives anything at all. By 85, the early claimer can be
ahead on total corpus despite the smaller monthly figure.

The usual analysis compares monthly amounts. It leaves out what the earlier money
does in the intervening years, and over an eight-year head start that omission is
substantial.

## What this depends on

Honestly: quite a lot. The 10% return is hypothetical, actual returns vary, and
losses are possible. The right claiming age depends on income needs, health,
longevity expectations, tax position and how much investment risk you are willing
to take.

Someone who needs the income at 62 has no decision to make. Someone in poor health
has a different calculation from someone with longevity in their family.

The point is narrower than "claim early": **the delayed-gratification argument is
usually presented as self-evident, and it is not.** It deserves to be worked out
with your own numbers.

## The thing not to do

Leave it unclaimed. Those contributions were yours. Whatever you decide about
timing, start by finding out what you are owed.

*The dollar amounts here are illustrations, not guaranteed benefits. Overseas
eligibility depends on your circumstances and SSA rules. Personal views for
educational purposes; not individualised financial, tax or legal advice.*
