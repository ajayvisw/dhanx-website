# RNOR: the tax window returning NRIs keep missing

An NRI moving back to India gets a transitional tax status that many people never
find out about until it has expired.

It is called **RNOR** — Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident — and for the period it
lasts, foreign income can receive very different treatment from that of an ordinary
Indian resident.

## Who qualifies

Either of two tests:

- You were a non-resident for **nine of the previous ten years**, or
- You were outside India for **729 days or more over the previous seven years**.

The calculation itself is mechanical. The part people miss is that the status is
time-limited and begins from your return.

## Why it matters

India taxes the worldwide income of ordinary residents. During the RNOR period,
certain income arising outside India is treated differently.

Consider someone returning with **$100,000 of accumulated gains** in US
instruments. The US no longer treats them as resident. India does — but the RNOR
status changes the treatment of that foreign gain.

At a rule-of-thumb 15%, that is roughly **$15,000** turning on the timing of a
single sale.

## The step-up most people overlook

The useful move is not simply selling.

If you want to keep the holding for the next thirty years, you can **sell and
immediately repurchase**. The position is unchanged, and the cost basis resets to
the current, higher level. Future gains are then measured from that new base rather
than from what you originally paid.

The hesitation is usually psychological — selling something you intend to own feels
like a step backwards. Economically it is not. You end the day holding the same
asset with a better starting point.

## Timing is the whole thing

This window exists for a defined period after returning, and it closes.

That makes the **date of your move** a planning variable rather than an
administrative detail. Decisions about when to sell appreciated foreign assets,
when to restructure accounts, and when to actually relocate are worth sequencing
together rather than separately.

## An important limit

RNOR does not make all income tax-free. Treatment depends on the source and nature
of each income stream and on your individual residency facts.

That is precisely why this needs a qualified tax professional looking at your
specific position — the numbers involved are usually large enough to justify the
conversation, and the window does not reopen.
