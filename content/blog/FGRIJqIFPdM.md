# Owner earnings: Buffett's correction to free cash flow

Warren Buffett introduced owner earnings to fix a specific problem with free cash
flow: **it treats all capital expenditure as equivalent, and it is not.**

## Two kinds of capex

**Maintenance capex** keeps the existing business running — replacing worn
equipment, refreshing facilities, offsetting obsolescence. Spending required simply
to stand still.

**Growth capex** funds expansion. A company that has not invested heavily for
twenty years and then commits tens of billions to a new direction is not spending
to maintain anything.

Free cash flow subtracts both. Owner earnings subtracts only maintenance.

The logic: growth capex is discretionary and intended to produce future earnings.
Charging it against the current year's earnings power understates what the existing
business generates.

## The arithmetic

Take a company with:

- Net income **$1,000**
- Depreciation (non-cash) **$100**
- Change in working capital **+$200**
- Maintenance capex **$100**
- Growth capex **$300**
- Total capex **$400**

**Free cash flow:** start with net income, add back depreciation, adjust for
working capital, subtract **all** capex.

$1,000 + $100 + $200 − $400 = **$900**

**Owner earnings:** the same, subtracting **maintenance capex only**.

$1,000 + $100 + $200 − $100 = **$1,200**

Free cash flow is typically the lower figure, and the gap is the growth investment.

## Why it matters for valuation

Buffett's argument is that owner earnings better represents what the business
actually earns, because it separates **what the current operation produces** from
**what management is spending to build something new**.

A company in a heavy investment cycle can show weak free cash flow while the
underlying business is performing well. Judged on free cash flow alone, it looks
expensive. Judged on owner earnings, it may not.

So when you look at a price-to-free-cash-flow multiple, consider
**price-to-owner-earnings** alongside it. On a business mid-build-out, the two can
tell opposite stories — and the difference is precisely the thing you want to form
a view on.

## The hard part

The split between maintenance and growth capex is rarely disclosed. Companies
report total capital expenditure, and separating it requires judgement about what
the business would need to spend simply to stay where it is.

That judgement is the work. It is also why the measure is useful — it forces you to
form a view on whether the spending is defending the business or extending it.
