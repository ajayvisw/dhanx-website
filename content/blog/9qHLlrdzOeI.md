# Lenskart IPO: what the valuation requires

Lenskart is listing at a market capitalisation around **₹70,000 crore** —
approximately **$8 billion**. The arithmetic needed to justify that is worth
working through.

## The starting numbers

FY2025 revenue: approximately **₹6,650 crore**.

Reported profit was around ₹297 crore, but after one-time adjustments the
**normalised profit is closer to ₹130 crore**. That is the number to use.

On ₹6,650 crore of revenue, that is a **net margin of about 2%**.

At a ₹70,000 crore valuation against ₹130 crore of normalised earnings, the
price-to-earnings ratio is approximately **536×**.

Worth pausing on that figure before going further.

## Where the money actually goes

The total IPO is about **₹7,278 crore**, but it splits in a way that matters.

**Fresh issue: roughly ₹2,100 crore.** This goes to the company, for operations and
growth.

**Offer for sale: about ₹5,100 crore.** This goes to existing shareholders —
institutional holders and founders selling down.

So of the money retail investors put in, **roughly 70% reaches existing owners
rather than the business**. That is a legitimate structure and a common one. It
simply means most of the capital raised does not fund the growth the valuation
requires.

## What would make 536× reasonable

Suppose you want the multiple to reach a more normal **25×**.

Earnings would need to rise by a factor of **21** — a cumulative increase of about
2,000%.

Over five years, that is approximately **84% compound annual growth in earnings**.

I am not aware of any company sustaining 84% earnings growth over five years —
certainly not one operating since 2010 with an established track record. Lenskart
is a good business. It is not a new technology changing customer behaviour
overnight.

## The revenue implied

Hold the 2% margin constant. Achieving 21× the current profit — roughly **₹2,800
crore** — would require revenue near **₹1,49,750 crore**.

Against ₹6,650 crore today. That is not a growth forecast; it is a different
company.

## Now be generous on margins

Assume operational improvements lift net margin from 2% to **10%** — a fivefold
increase, and a genuine achievement if delivered.

Even then, supporting a 25× multiple requires revenue roughly **four times current
levels**, implying about **33% compound annual growth for five years**.

33% sustained for five years is rare in any market. Very few companies of any size
manage it.

## The conclusion

The numbers do not leave much room for interpretation.

Even assuming substantial margin expansion that has not yet happened, the valuation
requires growth at a rate that almost nothing achieves. And the bulk of the capital
being raised goes to existing shareholders rather than funding that growth.

A good company and a good investment are different questions. This is the second
one.

*Personal analysis and opinion, not financial advice. Do your own research before
any investment decision.*
