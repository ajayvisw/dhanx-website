# OpenAI's nested web of deals, and who carries the risk

OpenAI sits at the centre of an unusual set of arrangements. Tracing where the
money flows — and who is exposed if it stops — is more revealing than any single
announcement.

## Three deployment models

**Self-run.** OpenAI owns the data centres end to end, buying chips from Nvidia and
AMD and operating them like a traditional hyperscaler.

**Oracle Cloud (OCI).** Oracle builds and operates the data centres using the same
chips. OpenAI has less control here — and Oracle retains fungibility, able to sell
spare capacity elsewhere if another buyer appears.

**Stargate.** A consortium of OpenAI, OCI and SoftBank. This gives OpenAI the
greatest control.

Nvidia supplies most of the silicon throughout, with AMD a smaller share it is
working to expand.

## Nvidia: selling and investing simultaneously

Nvidia has committed up to **$100 billion to OpenAI**.

Cash leaves Nvidia. But OpenAI then spends on Nvidia GPUs — and probably
considerably more than $100 billion as milestones are met.

It is a circular arrangement that benefits both, and Nvidia's risk is low. It is
investing in the demand for its own product, holding the strongest position in the
chain.

## AMD: warrants structured as an incentive

The AMD arrangement is more inventive.

AMD issued OpenAI warrants for roughly **160 million shares at one cent each** —
about **$1.6 million** of outlay. They vest only if **AMD's stock triples**.

AMD trades near $200. At $600, those shares would be worth approximately **$96
billion**.

Read what that structure actually does. AMD is saying: use our systems, and if that
adoption makes us succeed, you capture an enormous share of the upside. OpenAI is
saying: your products are credible enough that we will bet on them.

Both gain. AMD gets volume, credibility and a path to being more than a fringe
alternative to Nvidia. OpenAI gets a potentially vast payoff for almost no cash.

## Oracle: the exposed participant

Oracle is spending to build data centres for OpenAI directly, in addition to
Stargate — a commitment in the region of **$30 billion**.

Its **debt-to-equity ratio is around 420%**.

A company already heavily indebted is taking on more debt to become a credible
competitor to AWS, Azure and Google Cloud. That is a genuine strategic opportunity,
and it is being pursued from a stretched balance sheet.

SoftBank's role in Stargate is conventional venture financing — capital now,
returns much later, with the patience and the pockets to wait.

## Ranking the exposure

**Nvidia — low.** Best product, demand exceeding supply, next-generation parts
coming. It is investing in its own order book.

**AMD — low on this deal, high as a stock.** The warrant structure costs little and
could transform the business. But AMD already trades near **122× earnings**. For
the warrants to vest the stock must triple from there, and existing shareholders
would be diluted. A 122× multiple before any of that is the alarming part.

**Oracle — high.** 420% debt-to-equity, taking on more, concentrated in one
customer.

**OpenAI — the best position of all.** A company of its size with investors,
infrastructure partners and chip makers queuing to offer favourable terms is in a
remarkable spot.

## The thing to notice

The participant everyone is watching — OpenAI — carries the least downside. The
risk has been distributed to those financing the build, and it is concentrated
where balance sheets are weakest.

That is usually how these structures work, and it is why reading the deal matters
more than reading the announcement.
