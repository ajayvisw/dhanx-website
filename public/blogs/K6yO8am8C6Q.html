Price versus value: a stock can get cheaper without falling

A stock can go nowhere for years and still become a much better investment. In this short, I explain the difference between price and value using a simple example: if the share price stays flat but earnings continue to grow, the company’s P/E ratio falls and…

Indian large caps trade at roughly 19 times earnings, with the Nifty about 10% below its own five-year median. Blue chips are on sale relative to their own history.

Which raises a question worth sitting with: what happens if the index goes nowhere for five years?

The arithmetic

Companies keep earning. The market declines to acknowledge it. The price stays put.

At 5% annual earnings growth — as weak as FY2026 — a 19× multiple falls to about 15×.

At 8% growth, in line with Motilal Oswal's forecast for large caps, it falls to roughly 13×.

Same price. Higher earnings. Lower valuation.

A P/E of 13 would be the cheapest Indian blue chips have traded in their history.

Why this matters

Watching the price alone is misleading. A flat chart looks like nothing happening, when what is actually happening is that the asset is becoming cheaper every year relative to the profits it generates.

Value and price diverge, and the gap has to resolve somehow. Markets rarely let a historically low multiple sit quietly on a growing earnings base.

Nobody knows when that resolves, or who the buyers will be. But a company that keeps compounding earnings while its price stands still is not stagnating — it is getting cheaper, and eventually that is noticed.
