SpaceX at $1.75 trillion, compared with its peers

A look at Starlink, launches and defence work, with a comparison to major AI and technology companies.

On this page

  1. The financials
  2. What that forces
  3. Starlink carries the group
  4. The peer comparison
  5. Where I would look

SpaceX is expected to list within a month or two at approximately $1.75 trillion.

That figure is the first thing worth examining. A round led in December 2025 valued it near $800 billion — so the price has more than doubled in under a year.

Google, Fidelity, Sequoia, Ron Baron and employees holding long-vested options arrived at the earlier number. A public investor arrives at this one.

The financials

2025 revenue: about $18.7 billion, with company-reported adjusted EBITDA of $6.6 billion. Q1 2026 came in at roughly $4.6 billion. Revenue growth of approximately 33% year on year.

The growth is not the concern. The capital expenditure is.

Capex rose from $4.4 billion in 2023 to $10.1 billion, driven largely by the xAI combination. Free cash flow is negative $9 billion, against roughly $105 million of profit in 2023.

That trajectory does not reverse, because xAI remains inside the business.

What that forces

Musk now has to generate enough compute for xAI's own requirements and operate as a hyperscaler selling capacity to others — competing with Google, Amazon and Microsoft.

That is already happening: a reported arrangement with Anthropic at around $1.25 billion a month, renting surplus capacity from Colossus 1 and 2. Selling spare compute is the hyperscaler business model, and it is a reasonable use of the assets.

It also means SpaceX is now partly a cloud company, which is not what the valuation narrative describes.

Starlink carries the group

Starlink generated roughly $11.3 billion of revenue on about $4.1–4.7 billion of capex, with approximately 10.3 million subscribers.

It is the business keeping the consolidated picture afloat, and the only one likely to produce meaningful returns within three to five years. Most other initiatives are genuinely long-range.

The peer comparison

Four companies, three of them listing shortly:

Nvidia — market capitalisation about $5.4 trillion, revenue growing around 66% annually over five years to roughly $216 billion, with Q1 2026 growth near 86% excluding China. Trading around 33× trailing earnings.

For a business compounding at that rate, 33× is not expensive. The law of large numbers works against it, but every AI company is a customer — including SpaceX, OpenAI and Anthropic. They will reduce dependence at the margin, but the remaining market is enormous and highly profitable.

SpaceX — $1.75 trillion.

OpenAI — roughly $800 billion.

Anthropic — around $400 billion.

Where I would look

The question for SpaceX is whether a $1.75 trillion valuation reaches Nvidia-like levels within five years. Most of the bets supporting that are long-dated, with Starlink the exception.

Of the four, I find Anthropic the most compelling. A strong enterprise base, a firm position in the coding market, and expected GAAP profitability around 2028. Its reported growth is at a level that cannot persist — but it does not need to for the case to work at considerably lower rates.

Munger said of Musk: never underestimate someone who overestimates himself. That has been the pattern across his companies, Tesla included — persistently high valuations and a base of investors backing him personally.

For me, SpaceX and OpenAI go in the too-difficult pile. Anthropic is worth a closer look.

Personal analysis, not investment advice. Figures as reported at the time of recording.
