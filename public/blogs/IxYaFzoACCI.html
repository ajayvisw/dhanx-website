Who actually takes the loss when AI project debt goes wrong

AI is driving a huge data-center buildout—but the financing behind it may be riskier than it looks. Here’s an illustrative “horror story” scenario: $18B in project debt → $9B recovered from the data center → $3B recovered from Oracle → $6B still left exposed…

On this page

  1. The scenario
  2. Where it goes
  3. The question to ask

A short illustration of where risk lands when a data centre is financed through a special purpose vehicle rather than on a company's own balance sheet.

The numbers are hypothetical. The waterfall is not.

The scenario

  • $18 billion of project debt finances a data centre.
  • Efficiency gains reduce what that capacity is worth. The facility is eventually

worth $9 billion.

  • The tenant's guarantee is called, but the tenant is under pressure. Recovery

through proceedings yields $3 billion.

  • $6 billion remains unrecovered.

Where it goes

Moving debt into an SPV removes the liability from the conventional balance sheet of the company using the asset. It does not remove the economic risk — that has to land somewhere.

The order is roughly:

1. Equity holders in the vehicle — first loss, typically private equity. 2. Then, if the gap is larger than the equity: banks, bond funds, private credit funds, insurers, institutional investors.

The last group is the one worth noticing. A pension fund or insurance portfolio holding what looks like infrastructure credit is at the end of that chain, often without having formed a view on data centre utilisation or GPU efficiency.

The question to ask

Off-balance-sheet financing moves where a liability is recorded. It does not change who is exposed.

So the question on any of these structures is simple: who is the tenant, and what is their credit actually worth if the asset underperforms? A vehicle backstopped by a double-A rated company and one backstopped by a speculative-grade borrower are different instruments, however similar the buildings.

The fuller version — including the Meta and Oracle structures and why one traded at a discount — is in the financial engineering behind the AI build-out.

Educational hypothetical. Not a prediction about any company, and not investment advice.
