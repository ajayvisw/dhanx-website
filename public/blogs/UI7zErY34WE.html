Anthropic's S-1: ten things in the filing

Anthropic’s S-1 filing offers a rare look at the business behind Claude. In under three minutes, I break down its revenue growth, usage-based model, Claude Code spending, cloud partnerships, bets in cybersecurity and life sciences, and the unusual governance…

Anthropic's Form S-1, filed 26 October 2026, is the first detailed look at the business behind Claude. A few things stand out.

The growth

Revenue went from $41 million in 2023 to $16.3 billion in the first half of 2026.

That is the number everyone will quote, and it is genuinely without close precedent at this scale.

The model underneath it

Revenue is usage-based rather than seat-based. Customers pay for what they consume.

That cuts both ways. It scales immediately with adoption, without renegotiating contracts — and it falls immediately if usage drops or if per-unit prices decline, which in this market they reliably do.

Claude Code

Developer spending on Claude Code is called out separately in the filing, which tells you something about how the company views it — not a feature of the assistant but a business with its own trajectory.

Cloud partnerships

The filing details relationships with cloud providers. These are substantial multi-year compute commitments, and they are among the largest obligations the business carries.

They also make Anthropic a significant counterparty in the data-centre financing structures now underwriting much of the AI build-out.

The newer bets

Cybersecurity and life sciences both feature. These are domains where the work is high-value, the errors are expensive, and the customer will pay for capability rather than price.

Whether they become meaningful lines or remain exploratory is one of the more interesting open questions in the document.

The governance structure

The most unusual part of the filing, and the part least discussed.

The proposed arrangement departs considerably from a standard listing in terms of who ultimately controls the company. For a business whose stated purpose involves safety constraints on its own product, that structure is not incidental — it is arguably the thing that determines whether those commitments survive public market pressure.

What to weigh

Revenue from $41 million to $16.3 billion in under three years is remarkable. So is a governance structure designed to keep a listed company from being steered purely by its shareholders.

Which matters more probably depends on your time horizon.

Source: Anthropic, PBC, Form S-1, filed 26 October 2026. For education and discussion only; not investment advice. The filing is preliminary and its figures and plans may change.
