US estate tax: the 40% rule Indian investors overlook

Why international investors should understand US-situs asset rules and applicable tax treaties.

On this page

  1. What the exposure looks like
  2. Why it is so easy to miss
  3. What this does not mean

Indian investors have put billions into US securities over the past decade, and the returns have justified it. One rule rarely enters the decision, and it does not appear on any brokerage statement.

Estate tax is levied on an estate at death. For a non-US person holding US-situs assets, the rate can reach 40% — and the rules differ sharply from those applying to US citizens and residents.

What the exposure looks like

Take $100,000 invested today, compounding at roughly 14% a year — in line with what parts of the US technology sector have delivered. After twenty years that is about $1 million.

If the holder dies at that point, the estate could face US estate tax of approximately $330,000 — around a third of the position.

The same holding, with a US citizen as owner, could face nothing. The difference is not performance, residence or tax filings. It is the passport.

Why it is so easy to miss

The exposure is invisible during accumulation. It does not reduce returns, show up in annual filings, or appear on a statement. It materialises once, at the worst possible moment, and is borne by people who did not make the investment decision.

It also scales with success. A position that performs well becomes a larger liability — which inverts the usual intuition that a good investment needs less attention over time, not more.

For anyone holding US assets with a twenty- or thirty-year horizon, intending to pass wealth to the next generation, this belongs in the plan from the start rather than as a late correction.

What this does not mean

It is not an argument against investing in US markets. The diversification and the access to those businesses remain real.

It is an argument for understanding how the holding is structured before the position becomes large. Fund domicile, direct versus indirect ownership, residence, domicile and applicable treaty provisions can all change the answer — and they are far easier to arrange early than to unwind later.

Educational only, and not tax, legal or investment advice. Estate tax treatment depends on individual circumstances and applicable tax treaties. Take qualified cross-border tax and legal advice on your own position.
