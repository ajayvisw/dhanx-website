AI Is Booming. So Why Isn’t Indian IT?

TCS reports an AI business running at roughly $2.6 billion annually. HCLTech reports Advanced AI growing more than 60% year on year.

Meanwhile TCS, HCL, Wipro and their peers are growing total revenue in the low single digits.

Both sets of numbers are true. Reconciling them explains what AI is actually doing to the Indian IT model.

Where the money used to go

Take a traditional $100 million contract. Roughly $80 million went to headcount — infrastructure, development, maintenance of the stack. About $20 million went to technology and cloud.

The labour was the product. Scale meant more people.

Where it goes now

With AI tooling, the same scope needs perhaps $50 million of manpower rather than $80 million.

The missing $30 million does not vanish. It moves — to the cloud providers, the GPU vendors, and the model companies. AWS, Azure, OpenAI and Anthropic capture a larger share of the same customer budget.

So an Indian IT firm can grow AI revenue by 60% and still post low single-digit total growth, because the new revenue is replacing a larger amount of legacy revenue that AI has made unnecessary. Competition in infrastructure services is compressing the remainder.

This is why "AI revenue" as a headline is close to meaningless on its own. It measures the new line without netting the old one.

The metric that actually matters

If each customer now generates less revenue, the only route back to growth is more customers.

TCS's customer count is increasing at a single-digit rate year on year. That does not compensate for revenue compression per account — the arithmetic simply does not close.

Three indicators worth watching instead of AI revenue:

  • Is the customer count rising sharply year on year? Not steadily. Sharply.
  • Is revenue growing faster than headcount? If the two move together, AI is not

changing the operating model.

  • Is revenue per employee rising? This is the cleanest single measure of

whether the efficiency is being captured or competed away.

The real question

It is not whether AI destroys Indian IT. It is whether these firms can use AI to serve many more customers with fewer people each, before the old outsourcing economics compress underneath them.

That is a sales and delivery-model problem rather than a technology one. The tools are available to everybody. What is scarce is the ability to reach a far larger number of accounts at the lower revenue each now represents.
