Amazon's neglected businesses

A short review of Amazon’s competitive advantages and key businesses.

On this page

  1. AWS still leads, and the competitive gap is narrowing for a specific reason

  3. Healthcare is early and quiet

Amazon is discussed almost entirely as an AWS company. That framing misses where several of its durable advantages actually sit.

AWS still leads, and the competitive gap is narrowing for a specific reason

AWS remains the largest cloud provider, at roughly $107 billion in revenue. Rivals have posted faster growth rates, but from smaller bases.

One genuine headwind has been model exclusivity: leading proprietary models were available first on competing clouds. That is changing. Amazon is also investing heavily in its own silicon with Trainium, which addresses cost per unit of compute rather than model access.

The fulfilment network is the underrated asset

Amazon's fulfilment, physical and transportation infrastructure is arguably the best in the world, and it is rarely discussed as a competitive advantage in its own right.

The reason it matters is marginal cost. Launching a new business on top of infrastructure that already exists costs a fraction of building it standalone. Amazon piloted car purchasing with Hyundai and has since expanded the options — a new category layered onto a network built for something else.

Expect more of these. The infrastructure makes each successive one cheaper than the last.

Healthcare is early and quiet

One Medical and Amazon Pharmacy are small, in selected cities, and developing with little attention.

The proposition is the same one Amazon has executed in retail — convenience, price and value. Applied to pharmacy, even partial penetration represents a multi-billion dollar opportunity.

The margin story the market has not priced

This is the part most often missed.

Amazon's physical retail operating margin is around 6.5% — against roughly 4% at Walmart and about 3.5% at Costco. A business widely treated as a low-margin commodity operation is meaningfully more profitable than its closest comparators.

Free cash flow margin currently runs at about 5.2%, during a period of record AI infrastructure investment. Absent $30–35 billion of that spending, the figure would be nearer 10%.

And the trajectory: operating income rose from $22 billion in 2019 to roughly $70 billion in 2024.

What this adds up to

The AI capital expenditure is real and it suppresses current free cash flow. The argument is that it obscures rather than contradicts the underlying improvement — a retail operation outperforming its peers on margin, infrastructure that makes each new business cheaper to launch, and healthcare still at the beginning.
