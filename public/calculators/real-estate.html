Three property questions, three different calculators

"Is property a good investment" is really three separate questions, and they have different answers. Picking the wrong tool is how people end up with a confident number that does not address their situation.

I am deciding where to live

Rent vs Buy — you need somewhere to live and you are choosing between paying a landlord and paying a bank. It counts what the usual argument leaves out: stamp duty, registration, maintenance, and what your down payment would have earned had it stayed invested. On the default assumptions, buying needs about 9.2% annual appreciation to beat renting and investing the difference.

I am deciding where to invest

Real Estate vs Equities — you already have somewhere to live and this is about where the money goes. It matches the cash flows rupee for rupee: the equity path gets the same down payment and the same monthly outgo as a SIP. Because the property collects rent here, the bar is lower — about 7.5% appreciation.

I already own it and want the truth

Property XIRR — what has it actually returned, counting the down payment, every EMI, maintenance, taxes, rent received and today's value as dated cash flows. Usually well below the headline price move, and it reports both the unlevered return (the asset) and the levered one (your money), which can be lower when your loan rate exceeds the appreciation rate.

The three mistakes these tools exist to prevent

  • Comparing EMI against rent. It leaves out the largest item on the buying side, which is the return your down payment and stamp duty would have earned elsewhere.
  • Quoting the price move as the return. It ignores six to eight percent of acquisition cost, a decade of interest, maintenance and vacancy.
  • Assuming leverage always helps. It multiplies the gap between your asset's growth and your borrowing cost — and when the loan is dearer than the appreciation, that gap is negative.

Common questions

Which calculator should I use?

If you need somewhere to live, rent vs buy. If you have somewhere to live and are choosing an asset, real estate vs equities. If you already own the property and want to know how it has done, property XIRR. They answer different questions and will give different thresholds, which is correct rather than contradictory.

Why do rent-vs-buy and real-estate-vs-equities disagree?

Because in one you live in the home and pay rent to nobody, and in the other you let it out and collect rent from a tenant. Rental income materially helps the property side, which is why the appreciation needed to justify buying drops from about 9.2% to about 7.5%. Owning and letting is a better deal than owning and living — on the numbers.

Do these work for property outside India?

Yes. Switch the country and the defaults change — mortgage rates near 7%, transfer and title costs under 1% rather than 5–8% stamp duty, property tax as a meaningful annual percentage, and 9–11% for broad US equities. The structure of each comparison is identical; only the inputs move.

Do they account for tax?

Rent vs buy includes the home-loan interest deduction if you claim one. Capital gains and rental-income tax are not modelled anywhere, so treat every output as pre-tax. Depending on your bracket this can move results by a percentage point or two.

Do I need an account?

No. All three run entirely in your browser and nothing you type is sent to a server. A free account only adds the ability to save a scenario and open it on another device.
