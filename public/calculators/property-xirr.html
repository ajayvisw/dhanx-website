What has your property actually returned?

Almost nobody knows. The number people quote is the price move — "I bought at ₹80 lakh and it's worth ₹1.5 crore now" — which ignores the stamp duty, the ten years of EMIs, the maintenance, the property tax, the rent that did or did not come in, and the fact that the money arrived and left on dozens of different dates. Once you count all of it, the answer is usually a good deal smaller, and occasionally it is a shock.

The flat nearly doubled, and the return on the money you put in was 4.9% a year — below a fixed deposit. Meanwhile the property ate ₹40,316 a month in net cash: rent of ₹23,000 against an EMI of ₹57,600 and ₹5,733 of upkeep. For ten years.

The part that surprises people: leverage can cut your return

Everyone knows a loan amplifies gains. Fewer people notice it works in reverse. In the example above the unlevered return — what the property itself earned — was 7.1%. The levered return, on your own equity, was 4.9%. Lower, not higher.

The reason is simple once stated: the loan cost 9% and the asset grew at 6.5%. You borrowed at a rate above what the thing you bought was earning, so every rupee of debt dragged the result down. Leverage multiplies the gap between your asset's return and your borrowing cost, and that gap can be negative.

This is why "property always wins because of leverage" is only true when appreciation comfortably exceeds your mortgage rate. In most Indian metros over the last decade it has not.

Two numbers, and which one you want

  • Unlevered XIRR — what the asset earned, ignoring how you paid for it. Use this to judge the property as an investment, or to compare one property against another.
  • Levered XIRR — what your money earned, after the loan. Use this to compare against your equity portfolio, your FD, or anything else you could have done with the down payment.

If you only ever look at one, look at the levered number. It is the one that answers "was this a good use of my capital".

What gets counted

The down payment, stamp duty, registration, brokerage and interiors as the opening outflow; every EMI; maintenance and property tax monthly; rent net of vacancy, from whichever year it started; and the current value less any outstanding loan at exit. Each is dated, which is the whole point — ten years of monthly flows cannot be collapsed into a single percentage without losing the answer.

Common questions

Why is my property's XIRR so much lower than the price increase?

Because the price increase ignores everything else. Stamp duty and registration are 6–8% gone on day one. Interest front-loads: on a 20-year loan at 9%, most of the first decade's EMIs are interest, not principal. Maintenance and property tax run every year. And the rent, if any, usually falls well short of the EMI. The headline number is the one input that looks best in isolation.

What is the difference between levered and unlevered XIRR?

Unlevered treats the whole purchase price as your outflow and measures what the asset earned. Levered counts only the cash you actually put in — the down payment and acquisition costs — and then every EMI as it was paid, measuring what your own money earned. When your loan rate is above the appreciation rate, the levered figure is the lower of the two.

Should I include the rent I saved by living in it?

If you live in the property you receive no rent, so leave it unrented — the return will look worse, and it should, because you consumed the asset rather than renting it out. The rent you avoided paying is a real benefit, but it belongs in a rent-versus-buy comparison, not in the property's own return.

Does it handle a refinance?

Yes. Give the month you refinanced and the new rate, and the outstanding balance at that point is re-amortised on the new terms, with the EMI changing from that month onward. The cash flows before and after are both counted at their own dates.

What counts as a good XIRR on property?

Compare it to what the same money would have done elsewhere, not to an absolute. Indian equity indices have compounded in the low teens over long periods; a fixed deposit pays 6–7% with no maintenance, no vacancy and no stamp duty. A levered property XIRR below your loan rate means the leverage worked against you.

How many years do I need for the number to be meaningful?

At least twelve months, which the tool enforces. But property transaction costs are so heavily front-loaded that anything under about five years is dominated by them, and the XIRR will look dreadful almost regardless of what happened to the price.
